Tutorial for the Manual Process of the Letter/Tip Generation Application

The HIPAA Communication Toolkit creates clear, professional letters regarding healthcare concerns, as well as a section for communication tips. It is designed to help caregivers, patients, and family members communicate more effectively with providers.

The process asks you to select the choices from a series of questions that best represent your healthcare situation. A professionally written communication letter, fallback email, and personalized communication tips and guidance document are created.

Step 1 - Situation Identification

The first step asks the user to choose one of four available Communication Pathways. Each pathway represents a different scenario the user must include in their submission. They must choose the pathway that most closely resembles their situation.

Once this step is complete, the user will be sent to the next page, which will prompt additional questions.

Step 2 - Questionnaire

This page asks the user to respond to several questions. Their purpose is to further expand upon the user's situation.

Questions involving the Treatment Setting, Current Needs and Challenges, Actions Sought, Challenges, and Information to Share or Exchange request that the user select one or multiple checkboxes based on their response to the question. The user is also given the option to enter a custom response.

The Role and Relationship question only allows one answer per field. This question also gives the option to enter a custom response.

Once the questions have been answered, the responses should be collected so they can be inserted into the system prompt.

To manually fill in the system prompt, you must take the answers you have submitted into the form, and replace them with the corresponding category enclosed in {curly braces}. For example, you would replace {caregiver_name} with your response to the caregiver name question in the questionnaire.

Once this is done, review the system prompt, and make sure that there is no placeholder text in the final prompt. After this, you will be ready to submit the prompt into an LLM.

Step 4 - The AI Model

Before choosing an AI model to submit your system prompt, it is important to remember that different AI models produce writing of varying quality.

Our current recommendation is Claude, but you may also want to check the AI Leaderboard Arena to compare the current model rankings. Scroll down to the Text leaderboard to make an informed choice based on the current rankings.

https://arena.ai/leaderboard

Step 5 - Claude

In your web browser, open Claude at https://claude.ai/new . Paste the complete prompt with the placeholder text replaced by the options you selected.

Once you submit the prompt, Claude will generate the following:

  • A professional communication letter
  • A fallback email version of the professional communication letter (this only appears when applicable)
  • Communication guidance and tips based on the situation entered into the system prompt

Make sure to review the letter to verify that the names, dates, medical information, requested actions, and tone reflect your answers and how you wish to communicate. You may edit it yourself or ask Claude to revise it.

Preliminary Disclaimer about AI Generated Letter

In their current form, AI models will not always produce an accurate output. In fact, they may not produce the same output from two identical inputs. For this reason, this tool should never be used for legal advice.

Further, this tool should be used for educational purposes. If you do plan to use a generated letter, ensure that you read it carefully and check for its accuracy, as it cannot be guaranteed.

Save the Letter

Once you are satisfied with the output, you may copy the generated text (either by highlighting it and copying it to your clipboard or by clicking the "Copy Text" button that may be offered by the AI model).

Next, copy the text into your word processor of choice (Microsoft Word, Google Docs, etc.) and export the file as a PDF.

GENERATED LETTER

July 26, 2026

Re: Request for Collaboration on Safety Planning and Care Coordination for Jessica Olson

Dear Emergency Department Physician,

My name is Mary Olson, and I am the mother of Jessica Olson, currently being seen in your emergency department. I am writing to share important observations from home and to request your partnership in safety planning and coordination with any other providers who may
become involved in Jessica's care.

Over recent weeks, I have observed significant behavioral changes in Jessica that concern me deeply. I want to share these observations with your team so that they may be considered as part of her evaluation and care plan. I understand that Jessica may not currently recognize the extent of these changes herself, and I want to support
her safety and well-being during this difficult time.

I understand that under HIPAA, a healthcare provider may use professional judgment to share or discuss a patient's protected health information with a family member involved in that patient's care, and that this discretion is broadened when a patient is incapacitated or
not present to object (45 CFR 164.510(b)). I raise this only so that, if appropriate in your clinical judgment, you might feel able to share relevant updates with me as Jessica's mother and caregiver. I also understand that I am always able to share information with you,
regardless of any privacy restriction on your end, and I hope this letter serves that purpose.

Jessica's safety, and the possibility of coordinating with any follow-up providers, are my primary concerns right now. I want to work collaboratively with your team to ensure she receives the support she needs.

Next steps: I would welcome the opportunity to speak with a member of the care team at your earliest convenience to share my observations in more detail and to discuss safety planning and any recommended follow-up care. Thank you for your attention to Jessica's well-being.

Sincerely,

Mary Olson
Mother of Jessica Olson

GENERATED COMMUNICATION TIPS

Tips for Talking with Providers:

Before the conversation, write down specific, dated examples of behavioral changes you've observed (what changed, when, how often) — concrete details are more useful to clinicians than general impressions.
Frame your input as "here's what I'm observing at home" rather than a diagnosis — this keeps the conversation collaborative and non-adversarial.
Remember that you can always give information to a provider — HIPAA restricts what providers disclose to others, not what you can tell them (45 CFR 164.510(b) also allows providers, at their discretion, to listen to and factor in caregiver input).
If your loved one seems unable to recognize their symptoms (anosognosia), gently let the provider know this, as it may affect how they approach safety planning and follow-up.
Ask directly: "Is there a safety plan in place, and how can I support it at home?"
It's normal to feel scared or overwhelmed in the ED — take a moment to ground yourself before the conversation, and don't hesitate to ask staff to repeat or clarify information.

I understand that under HIPAA, a treating provider may use
professional judgment to share relevant health information with a
family member who is involved in a patient's care (45 CFR 164.510(b)).
I raise this only to note that, where clinically appropriate, I would
welcome any information you are able to share regarding Vivian's
medications and how she is responding to treatment, so that I can best
support her.

My hope is to work together with your team so that Vivian's treatment
is adjusted in a way that reflects both her clinical needs and what we
have observed of her history outside the hospital setting.

Next steps: I would appreciate the opportunity to speak with you or a
member of the treatment team to share Vivian's medication history and
to discuss any adjustments being considered. Thank you for your care of
Vivian.

Sincerely,

John Wu
Father of Vivian Wu

GENERATED COMMUNICATION TIPS

Tips for Talking with Providers

1. Prepare a simple written timeline of medications, dosages, and any side effects you've observed - this saves time and reduces the chance of details being missed in conversation.

2. Ask specifically: "Are there medication changes being considered, and what side effects should we watch for?"

3. If communication with your loved one is limited right now, let the psychiatric team know this directly - it helps them understand where information gaps exist.

4. You can share adherence history and observations with the provider at any time; HIPAA governs what the provider discloses to others, not what you can tell them.

5. If you'd like updates going forward, ask the team what the best process is (e.g., a specific point of contact or scheduled check-in call).

6. Keep requests specific and time-bound ("Can we talk for 10 minutes this week?") - busy inpatient teams often respond better to concrete, manageable asks.
